The throwback

Before programming, I was not sure what my talent or skill was. This is how one classroom experience slowly changed that.

01 • Before

I did not know my direction yet.

In Grade 9, I did not really know what skill I had, what I enjoyed, or what I wanted to pursue. Academics did not feel important to me yet.

02 • The choice

I moved into ICT.

When I learned there was an opening in ICT/TLE, I chose it without hesitation. At first, I still had no idea what I was going to discover there.

03 • The spark

Then Sir started teaching programming.

Programming was unfamiliar to me. Sir introduced Python and explained it in a way that made me curious instead of afraid of the lesson.

04 • The challenge

The project looked difficult.

We were given a calculator project with a structure, but our lessons were not yet enough for me to understand every part. Some class time was also affected by schedules and other responsibilities.

05 • The first project

So I studied until I could finish it.

I used AI as a study tool to understand the project and learn how to build it. The next day, I continued working, fixed a problem with percentage handling, and finally completed the calculator.

Presentation note: This website is a “vibe-coded” presentation made to show the story. It is not being presented as my main production work. I have also built a separate real Python backend system as part of my actual programming practice.

The moment that stayed

A small classroom project became a much bigger realization.

THE MOMENT

“Sir, tapos na po ako.”

I finished the project, told Sir, and fixed the remaining issue I had with the percentage. It was a simple moment, but it gave me confidence.

THE CLASS

My classmates started asking how I did it.

One classmate asked how I made the project and whether they could use my code. Later, I realized other classmates were using it as a guide too. Some changed parts of their versions, but we all managed to get through that day.

THE REALIZATION

“Ah, ganito pala. Programming might be for me.”

That was the point where programming stopped being just another school lesson. It became something I wanted to keep learning.

THE TEACHER LEFT • THE LESSON STAYED

After Grade 10, Sir moved to another school.

Even though Sir was no longer in our school, what I learned from those first lessons stayed with me. I continued studying, building, making mistakes, and improving.

A throwback letter • from one of your former students

Dear Sir Leo Higuit Grefaldo,

I do not know if you still remember me clearly, but I am Jerosh Nevado, one of your students from Grade 9. I made this small project because I wanted to give you something different from a normal message.

I wanted to bring you back for a moment to the classroom where I first discovered programming.

Back then, I honestly did not know what my talent was. I did not know what my strength was, what skill I had, or what I wanted to become. I was not even very interested in academics. There were times when a lesson could be happening in front of me and I simply did not know why I should care about it.

Then Grade 9 came, and I had the chance to move into ICT. I took that chance without really knowing what was waiting for me. Then you started teaching us programming.

I remember how unfamiliar it felt at first. Programming was something completely new to me, and I did not know how to build anything. But the way you taught it gave me a different feeling. Little by little, I became curious. Python was new, but instead of feeling like just another school subject, it started to feel like something I wanted to understand.

I also remember the project days. Sometimes our lessons could not continue because of schedules and other responsibilities, and then suddenly we would have a project to make. One of the first projects I remember was the calculator.

At that time, I did not really know how to make it. Our lesson was not yet enough for me to understand everything. So instead of stopping, I studied. I used AI as a study tool to help me understand the project, and I tried to figure out how the parts worked.

The next day, I opened the computer and continued until I finished it. I remember telling you, “Sir, tapos na po ako.” There was still a small problem with the percentage handling, but I fixed it and completed the project.

Then one of my classmates asked me how I made it and if they could copy the code. I said yes. I did not even realize at first that other classmates were also using it as a guide. Some changed parts of their versions, but somehow, we all survived that day.

Looking back now, that moment was more important to me than I realized. It was not just about finishing a calculator.

It was the moment I started thinking, “Ah, ganito pala. Maybe programming is for me.”

That was one of the first times I felt that I had found a skill that I actually wanted to continue.

After Grade 10, I learned that you had already left our school and were teaching somewhere else. That was when I realized even more how thankful I was that you became my teacher. You may have only been teaching us programming in a classroom, but for me, you helped open a direction that I did not know I had.

I am still learning now. I have made my own projects, including a real Python backend system, and I am still studying because I know I have a lot more to learn. This website is only a presentation project, but I made it because I wanted to show you something simple: the student you once taught is still here, still coding, and still continuing the lesson.

Maybe when you were teaching us back then, you did not know how much one lesson would stay with one of your students. I did not know either.

But now I do.

So thank you, Sir.

Thank you for introducing me to programming when I did not even know that programming could become part of my life. Thank you for being one of the teachers who helped me discover something about myself.

Someday, when I become the programmer I am working toward becoming, I hope I can look back and remember that the journey started with a simple classroom lesson, a difficult first project, and a teacher who gave me my first spark.

I just wanted to give you a little throwback from one of your former students. You taught the lesson years ago, but I am still writing the next lines.

With respect and gratitude,
Jerosh Nevado
The student who found his direction through programming.
